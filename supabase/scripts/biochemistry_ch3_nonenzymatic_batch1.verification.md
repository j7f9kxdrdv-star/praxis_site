**Stem:** A researcher studies vesicle trafficking in a cultured neuron whose axonal microtubules are oriented with their plus ends directed toward the distal axon terminal. Assuming dynein activity is unchanged, which movement is most likely to be selectively impaired in the treated neuron?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §5.5.6, lines 23238-23239: 'These motor proteins are kinesin, which moves cargo towards the (+) end, and dynein, which moves it to the (-) end.' Also lines 23262-23263: kinesins 'were involved in cargo (organelles, mRNAs, proteins) transport along the long axons of neuronal cells.' **Distractor citation 1:** Choice B wrongness — lines 23239 & 23300: dynein 'moves it to the (-) end' and dyneins 'move towards the minus end of microtubules'; movement from the terminal back toward the cell body is minus-end-directed (dynein), not kinesin, and dynein is unchanged.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C wrongness — lines 23244-23245 & 23300: 'Kinesin moves towards the microtubule's plus end, whereas the dyneins move towards the minus end'; retrograde transport toward the perinuclear/minus end is a dynein function and is therefore spared.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrongness — lines 23278-23279: kinesin-related proteins act 'in moving microtubules during mitosis'; a differentiated post-mitotic neuron has no mitotic spindle, so this process is absent and cannot be the selectively impaired movement.

**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B (kinesin on actin): §5.5, line 22754-22757: "the three major classes of motor proteins: myosins (actin-based), kinesins, and dyneins (microtubule-based)... kinesin and dynein convert chemical energy... into directed movement along microtubule tracks" — kinesin is microtubule-based, so depolymerizing microtubules stops it.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C (dynein on actin): §5.5, line 22754-22757: kinesins and dyneins are "microtubule-based"; and line 878-879: "dynein and kinesin (that move along tubulin microtubules)" — dynein requires microtubules, so its transport stops.

### Q3 — Motor Proteins (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher studies vesicle transport driven by a microtubule-based motor protein in a living cell. She introduces a metabolic poison that rapidly blocks ATP regeneration, and cytoplasmic ATP falls to a negligible level while all motor proteins and filaments remain structurally intact. Which outcome for the motor-driven transport of the vesicle is best predicted by the mechanism of this class of proteins?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt lines 23307-23309: "Movement and force generation by both classes of proteins involves ATP hydrolysis and allosteric shifts in the orientation of the motor domains so that the proteins are thought to 'step' progressively down the microtubule." Also lines 13525-13526: the motor domain "binds and hydrolyzes ATP, providing the free energy that powers muscle contraction." Therefore removing ATP eliminates the energy for the power-stroke conformational cycle and directed transport halts (Choice A).  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B wrongness — libretexts-biochemistry.txt lines 23308-23309 state movement requires ongoing "ATP hydrolysis" cycling to step; a single static bound ADP cannot repeatedly power strokes, so continued reduced-speed transport on bound ADP alone is unsupported.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C wrongness — libretexts-biochemistry.txt lines 23373-23374: "When ADP is released, the central β-sheet twists, leading to binding to microtubules... microtubules act to exchange ADP/ATP," and line 23388: "Kinesin is permanently attached to the microtubule by either one head or the other." It is nucleotide (ATP) exchange, not ATP absence, that governs release; without ATP the head does not simply detach and let cargo freely diffuse away.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrongness — libretexts-biochemistry.txt lines 23270-23273 and 23353-23356 attribute direction to filament polarity and the configuration/coordination of the motor domains, not to ATP level; removing ATP stalls the ATP-driven forward cycle rather than reversing travel direction.

### Q6 — Binding Proteins (hard, Skill 4)
**Stem:** A researcher measures fractional O2 saturation (Y) versus partial pressure of O2 (\(P_{O_2}\)) for a purified tetrameric globin under two buffer conditions. Both curves are S-shaped, and the two curves are nearly superimposable in steepness (their maximum slopes are equal), but the entire curve for Condition 2 lies to the right of the curve for Condition 1. The \(P_{O_2}\) at which Y = 0.5 is 26 mm Hg in Condition 1 and 40 mm Hg in Condition 2. Which conclusion is best supported by comparing these two data sets?

| Condition | \(P_{O_2}\) at Y = 0.5 | Curve shape |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------|
| 1 | 26 mm Hg | S-shaped |
| 2 | 40 mm Hg | S-shaped |  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §5.03, lines 20617–20618: "The oxygen concentration at which Y = 0.5 is defined as P50" (operational definition of KD; higher P50 = lower affinity). Line 20436–20437: allosteric ligands "shift the binding curves of Hb for oxygen to the right, lowering the apparent affinity of Hb for oxygen." Line 27893: increasing "KD (lower affinity) would reveal a series of identical sigmoidal curves shifted progressively to the right" — a pure affinity change translates the curve rightward while preserving its sigmoidal (cooperative) shape.  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B (reversed): Line 20487 — "Decreasing pH shifts the oxygen binding curves to the right (to decreased oxygen affinity)"; a rightward shift / higher P50 is decreased affinity, so calling it increased affinity reverses the true relationship.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C (misconception): Line 20391–20392 — the sigmoidal shape itself is what "suggest[s] cooperative binding"; line 27893 shows an affinity change keeps the curves "identical" in sigmoidal shape, so abolishing cooperativity (which would flatten the S toward a hyperbola, line 20418) is not indicated by a mere rightward shift with unchanged steepness.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D (partial truth): Line 20617–20618 defines P50 as the affinity measure; since P50 rose from 26 to 40 mm Hg, affinity did change, so claiming affinity is unchanged contradicts the data, and stronger site interaction would change steepness rather than simply translate the curve.  

### Q7 — Binding Proteins (easy, Skill 1)
**Stem:** A researcher studies a cytosolic sensor protein with two distinct regions: one region reversibly grips free \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\) ions, and a separate region docks onto a target kinase. In resting cells the sensor floats free of the kinase, but when intracellular \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\) rises, the \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\)-loaded sensor clamps onto the kinase and the kinase becomes active. The researcher then mutates only the \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\)-gripping region so that it can no longer bind \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\), leaving the kinase-docking region intact. When intracellular \(\text{Ca}^{2+}\) is raised in cells expressing this mutant sensor, what is the most likely outcome for the kinase?  
**Answer:** B · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §5.6.3 (line 23753): "This calcium-binding protein binds amphiphilic helical peptides, leading to a large conformation change in the protein." — the Ca2+-binding protein (calmodulin) binds Ca2+ and then engages/brings together a target peptide/protein; §4.4.1.1 (lines 11760-11762): "These are found in DNA-binding proteins that regulate transcription and calcium-binding proteins, the motif of which is often called the EF-hand," establishing binding proteins bring molecules (Ca2+, target) together to exert function.  
**Distractor citation 1:** A (reversed relationship): §5.6.3 (line 23753) shows the Ca2+-loaded sensor engages its target upon binding (activation), so the sensor does not act as an inhibitory brake whose loss activates the kinase.  
**Distractor citation 2:** C (ligand acts directly): §5.6.3 (line 23753) states the calcium-binding protein itself binds the target peptide only after binding Ca2+, so the binding protein is required to link Ca2+ to the target—Ca2+ does not act on the target without it.  
**Distractor citation 3:** D (covalent modification): §5.1.14 (lines 19430-19441) and §4.4.1.1 (lines 11760-11762) describe binding proteins forming noncovalent complexes (e.g., lac repressor/operator DNA, EF-hand gripping Ca2+), i.e., they associate noncovalently and do not covalently modify their partners.  

### Q8 — Immunoglobulins (medium, Skill 1)
**Stem:** A purified antibody migrates as a single ~150 kDa species on non-reducing SDS-PAGE. When the same sample is run with a reducing agent that cleaves disulfide bonds, it resolves into exactly two bands, one at ~50 kDa and one at ~25 kDa, with the ~25 kDa band staining about twice as intensely as would be expected from a single copy. Which conclusion about the antibody's architecture is best supported by this result?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §4.3.4 Quaternary Structure, lines 11244-11248: "Most protein subunits in a larger protein displaying quaternary structure are held together by noncovalent interactions (intermolecular forces). However, in some cases, disulfide bonds also hold them together (an example includes immunoglobulins)." Combined with §3.3 SDS-PAGE, lines 7499-7500: "A multimeric protein whose subunits are held together by disulfide bonds can be resolved into component subunits by adding a reducing agent." These establish that immunoglobulins are held together by interchain disulfide bonds and separate into component chains only upon reduction.  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B wrongness — §3.2 Disulfide Bonds, lines 5951-5953: "When a protein folds, two Cys side chains might approach each other and form an intra-chain disulfide bond. Likewise, two Cys side chains on separate proteins might approach each other and form an inter-chain disulfide... disulfides are typically cleaved, and the chains are separated." Reduction cleaves disulfides (not peptide bonds), so a single continuous chain cannot be fragmented into two molecular-weight species.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C wrongness — §3.2 Disulfide Bonds, lines 5953-5955: "The disulfides can be cleaved by reducing agents such as beta-[mercaptoethanol]"; reducing agents act on \(-S-S-\) bonds, not the polypeptide backbone, so they cannot nick a ~75 kDa chain into ~50 and ~25 kDa pieces.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrongness — §3.3 SDS-PAGE, lines 7500-7502: "If the subunits are held together only by noncovalent interactions, they will separate under denaturing conditions (SDS), eliminating subunit interactions in the presence or absence of β-ME." A species that remains 150 kDa without βME but splits with βME must be covalently disulfide-linked, not noncovalently held.  

### Q9 — Immunoglobulins (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher incubates a suspension of live bacteria with purified antibodies raised against a surface protein found across the entire bacterial cell wall, so that the intact bacteria become densely coated with bound antibody. The antibodies used lack any ability to recruit or fix serum complement proteins. When these antibody-coated bacteria are then added to a culture of macrophages, the macrophages clear the bacteria from the medium far more rapidly than they clear an identical suspension of uncoated bacteria. Which change in the macrophages' handling of the bacteria best accounts for this faster clearance?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** LibreTexts Biochemistry (libretexts-biochemistry.txt) §3.3.5.4 Affinity Chromatography, lines 7031-7039: 'the chromatography resin is derivatized with a group that binds to a specific site on a given protein of interest ... an antibody that recognizes a specific amino acid sequence (an epitope) on a protein ... antibodies can be made that will bind selectively to a single protein' — establishes that an antibody functions by binding selectively to a specific epitope on a target; §3.5.1 ELISA, lines 9666-9671: 'the antibodies may ... capture an unlabeled antigen and use a second labeled antibody to detect the captured antigen ... three antibodies must bind the antigen to produce a reporter molecule' — establishes the core immunoassay logic that a bound antibody on a target is itself recognized/bound by a second agent, the exact recognition-of-the-bound-coat principle underlying opsonization (bound antibody coat is recognized by the phagocyte).  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B (direct lysis): The text describes antibody action exclusively as selective binding/recognition of an epitope (lines 7033-7038, 9651: "antibodies' ability to bind other molecules"), with no lytic or catalytic activity attributed to antibody binding; an antibody binding an epitope cannot itself rupture a cell wall.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C (blocking attachment): The text frames antibody binding as creating a recognizable bound complex that a second agent detects/acts upon (secondary-antibody logic, lines 9667-9671), not as a step that prevents the target from contacting the effector; blocking the surface protein would reduce, not speed, macrophage engagement.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D (toxin neutralization): The text establishes antibodies bind a specific antigen/epitope (lines 7033-7038); neutralizing a separate soluble toxin is a distinct binding target and would not alter how readily the bacterial cells themselves are bound and engulfed by macrophages, so it cannot explain faster clearance of the bacteria.

Combined with §4.4.1.1 (line 11761): the helix-turn-helix motif is "found in DNA-binding proteins that regulate transcription," and §5.x (line 41051): "The 5'-end promoter is where transcription factors and RNA polymerase assemble before transcription starts." ### Q11 — Ion Channels (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher records single-channel currents from three ion channels, each reconstituted alone in its own membrane patch, under three conditions. Channel X conducts steadily whenever the patch is held at −70 mV with no other manipulation, and its open probability is unchanged by a step to +20 mV or by adding the neurotransmitter acetylcholine. Channel Y stays closed at −70 mV and remains closed when acetylcholine is added, but conducts robustly the instant the patch is stepped to +20 mV. Channel Z stays closed at −70 mV and at +20 mV, and conducts only while acetylcholine is present in the bath. The researcher then engineers a mutant in which the charged residues of one channel's S4 transmembrane segment are neutralized. For which channel would this specific mutation most likely abolish the channel's ability to switch from a closed to a conducting state?

Channel X — conducts at −70 mV; unaffected by voltage step or acetylcholine
Channel Y — closed until stepped to +20 mV; unaffected by acetylcholine
Channel Z — closed until acetylcholine is added; unaffected by voltage step  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3B · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §11.3 lines 48265-48266: 'Segment (helix) 4 of each of the four repeat units illustrated above is the conserved "voltage" sensor. It contains multiple charged amino acids whose disposition changes with changes in the transmembrane potential, allowing conformational changes in the protein and gating of ion flow.' Also lines 48566-48567: a voltage-gated channel has 'a basal closed state found at resting cell potentials' and 'an open state found when a depolarizing potential is reached in the cell' — matching Channel Y (closed at −70 mV, opens on step to +20 mV, ligand-insensitive).  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B wrongness — line 48079: the leak channel is a 'voltage-insensitive potassium leak channel'; a voltage-insensitive channel has no S4-based voltage sensor to neutralize, so Channel X's constant conductance cannot depend on charged residues sensing the potential. Line 48874: such channels are 'always potentially active (open)' and (line 48876) gated 'not by changes in the transmembrane potential.'  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C wrongness — lines 47913-47914: for ligand-gated channels 'The ligand (neurotransmitter) binds to the extracellular domain... On ligand binding, TM2 and TM3 rearrange to allow the formation of a transient pore'; opening is driven by ligand binding to the extracellular orthosteric site (lines 47916-47918), not by movement of S4 voltage-sensor charges, so neutralizing S4 would not abolish Channel Z's gating.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrongness — line 48079: the leak channel is explicitly 'voltage-insensitive'; lines 47869-47870 list gating mechanisms ('ligand binding, change in membrane potential, lipid interactions, and mechanical stress') as distinct, so an ungated leak channel does not rely on the charged voltage sensor that voltage-gated channels use.  

### Q12 — Ion Channels (medium, Skill 2)

Compared with an untreated neuron immediately after a single stimulus, what is the most likely consequence of the toxin in the treated neuron?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3B · **Confidence:** 5

**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C wrong — §11.3.3 line 48567: the channel has 'an open state found when a depolarizing potential is reached in the cell,' and lines 48466-48467 state this state allows 'inward movement of Na+ ions.' The open state is the conducting state, so a pore held open conducts continuously; it is not silent until the voltage sensor moves.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrong — §11.3.3 lines 48466-48467 and 48478-48484: opening the channel permits Na+ influx that 'depolarize[s] a neuron'; holding the pore open drives depolarizing current rather than blocking the pore, so it does not prevent the membrane from reaching a depolarizing potential.

### Q14 — Ion Channels (easy, Skill 1)
**Stem:** A researcher studies a resting cell in which the concentration of K+ is 140 mM inside and 5 mM outside. A membrane protein opens to form a continuous aqueous pore, and K+ begins to move through it. Adding a chemical that halts all ATP production in the cell has no effect on this K+ movement. Which statement best accounts for the observed behavior of this protein?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3B · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** libretexts-biochemistry.txt §11.2.4, lines 47514-47516: "This process is called facilitated diffusion and represents a physical as opposed to chemical process since no covalent bonds are made or broken. **Distractor citation 3:** Choice D wrongness (reversed direction AND falsely requires ATP): combines the errors refuted by lines 47516 (direction is outward) and 47606-47608 (ATP requirement belongs to active transport, not channel-mediated facilitated diffusion).  

### Q15 — Receptor Enzymes (medium, Skill 1)
**Stem:** A receptor tyrosine kinase spans the plasma membrane once, with its ligand-binding region facing the extracellular space and its catalytic region in the cytoplasm. To probe how this receptor is switched on, an investigator treats cells with a bivalent artificial crosslinker that clamps two receptor monomers together in the membrane. Crucially, this is done in a ligand-free medium, so no natural ligand is present. Compared with untreated cells in the same ligand-free medium, what is the most likely effect on the receptor's intracellular catalytic activity?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3B · **Confidence:** 5
