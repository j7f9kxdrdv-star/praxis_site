### Q2 — Oxidation & Reducing Sugars (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher synthesizes two disaccharides from the same pair of hexose monomers. In Disaccharide I, the two rings are joined by an oxygen bridge that connects the anomeric carbon of one ring to the C4 hydroxyl of the other ring. In Disaccharide II, the two rings are joined by an oxygen bridge that directly connects the anomeric carbon of one ring to the anomeric carbon of the other ring. When each is warmed with an alkaline cupric-ion solution, only one of them causes the blue solution to form a brick-red precipitate. Which statement best explains why the two disaccharides behave differently?  
**Answer:** B · **Foundation:** 1D · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** LibreTexts Biochemistry, 7.1 Monosaccharides and Disaccharides: 'A monosaccharide, disaccharide, or even polysaccharide with at least one hemiacetal link (for instance, lactose) is a reducing sugar, as the monomer with the cyclic hemiacetal can reversibly open to form an aldehyde. However, if the only links in sugars are full acetals (such as in sucrose, when the link is between the two anomeric carbons), the sugar is not reducing.' Also: reducing sugars reduce a basic blue solution of CuSO4 (Cu2+) to a brick-red precipitate of Cu2O (Cu1+).  
**Distractor citation 1:** (B) LibreTexts 7.1: reducing behavior is due to the cyclic hemiacetal reversibly opening 'to form an aldehyde' that is oxidized — not to hydrolysis of the acetal glycosidic bond; the mild cupric reagents 'are mild and react with aldehydes, but not with ketones,' and act by ring-opening, not linkage cleavage.  
**Distractor citation 2:** (C) LibreTexts 7.1: a link through the anomeric carbon forms a full acetal ('the newly formed OH... acetal'), so a C1-to-C4 anomeric linkage is an acetal at C1, not a hemiacetal; the oxidant reacts with the exposed aldehyde, contradicting the claim that the acetal bond itself is oxidized.  
**Distractor citation 3:** (D) LibreTexts 7.1: sucrose, whose two anomeric carbons are joined (Glc(α1,2β)Fru), 'is not in equilibrium with its straight-chain keto form; hence, sucrose is a nonreducing sugar' — a carbon locked as a full acetal cannot open or tautomerize, so the C1-to-C1 disaccharide is the non-reducing one.

When this product is dissolved in fresh water and its behavior is compared with that of ordinary D-glucose, which property that glucose displays is NOT observed for the product?

A. It hydrogen-bonds extensively with water and remains highly soluble.
B. It rotates the plane of plane-polarized light passing through its solution.

**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 1D · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** LibreTexts Biochemistry, 7.1 Monosaccharides and Disaccharides (lines 34084–34086): a monosaccharide with at least one hemiacetal 'can reversibly open to form an aldehyde'; this reversible ring opening through the anomeric hemiacetal is the basis of both anomer interconversion (mutarotation) and reducing behavior. Index entry 'mutarotation' at line 51385 anchors the term. ### Q9 — Keto-Enol Tautomerism (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher dissolves a sample of pure D-glucose in a dilute sodium hydroxide solution and lets it stand at room temperature. After several hours, chromatographic analysis of the solution reveals that the original sugar is now accompanied by two additional monosaccharides, all present at equilibrium. Assuming no bonds other than those at C1 and C2 are disturbed during the process, which set of monosaccharides would the chromatogram most likely show?  
**Answer:** C · **Foundation:** 1D · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** LibreTexts Biochemistry, 7.1 Monosaccharides and Disaccharides (lines 34081–34084): sugars that 'have an α-hydroxymethyl ketone group which can isomerize to an aldehyde under basic conditions (such as fructose)' — establishing that under basic conditions a 2-ketose and its corresponding aldose interconvert. Combined with the epimer definition at lines 33772–33795 ('epimers: stereoisomers that differ at 1 chiral center'; D-glucose vs. D-mannose are C2 epimers, D-glucose vs. D-galactose are C4 epimers) and the grouping of D-glucose, D-mannose, D-galactose, and D-fructose as related hexoses (line 33695), the common C1–C2 enediol links D-glucose, D-fructose, and D-mannose.  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice A (glucose/fructose/galactose): LibreTexts lines 33772 and 33791 define epimers as differing at one chiral center and identify D-galactose as the C4 (not C2) epimer of D-glucose; a C1–C2 enediol cannot alter C4, so galactose is not produced.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice B (glucose/mannose/galactose): LibreTexts lines 34081–34084 state the isomerization proceeds through the ketose form (fructose) under basic conditions; a set with no ketose omits the required intermediate, and galactose (C4 epimer, line 33791) is again unreachable via the C1–C2 enediol.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D (glucose/fructose only): LibreTexts lines 34081–34084 establish aldose↔2-ketose isomerization, but the epimer definition (line 33772) plus the C2-epimer relationship of glucose/mannose (line 33695) show the shared enediol also yields the C2 epimer D-mannose, so limiting the set to two sugars is incomplete.

**Distractor citation 3:** Choice C: LibreTexts Biochemistry lines 25444, 32813-32815 explicitly state the ketone is more stable than the enol; this choice reverses that ordering by labeling the enol as lower in free energy.

C. The anomeric carbon is converted to an ester, an ester bond forms to the methanol oxygen, and one molecule of water is released.

**Answer:** B · **Foundation:** 1D · **Confidence:** 5

### Q16 — Glycoside Formation & Hydrolysis (medium, Skill 2)

Which molecule accumulates as a byproduct of extending the chain by one glucose?
