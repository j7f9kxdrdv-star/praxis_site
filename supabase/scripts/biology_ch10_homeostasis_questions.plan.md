# Biology Chapter 10 — Homeostasis (Excretory System + Skin): Chapter Plan

## RECOMMENDED QUESTION COUNT: **57**

Hard floor 57. Defensible ceiling 60 (only if the founder promotes 3 reserve items). **Not 75. Not 90. Not 100.**

### How I got there

I did not start from a target. I enumerated distinct **answer-turning points** — the thing a key actually resolves on — that survive *both* the scope verdict and the boundary audit. A concept that survives scope but is owned elsewhere contributes **zero**, no matter how central it feels to the chapter. A concept that supports two genuinely different keys contributes two.

That enumeration produced **57 keys**: 46 excretory, 11 skin. A further **5 are held in reserve** (listed below); promoting three of them takes the chapter to 60 and makes the batch grid come out even. I am recommending 57 rather than 60 because the last three reserve items are the weakest in the chapter, and the Ch6 precedent says the cost of carrying weak items is not one weak item — it is a deduplication marathon, because every round of forcing distinctness in a saturated space spawns new collisions.

### Why the naive number is so much bigger than the real one

A Kaplan-shaped Homeostasis chapter is a 95–110 concept chapter. This one is raided from six directions at once:

- **Bio Ch5 Endocrine (100 questions)** takes the entire hormone axis.
- **Bio Ch7 Cardiovascular** takes Starling forces, the renal portal arrangement, efferent arteriolar resistance, filtration fraction, peritubular reabsorption, and all blood-pressure control.
- **Bio Ch6 Respiratory** takes skin thermoregulation *and* renal acid-base compensation.
- **GenChem Ch10** takes the bicarbonate buffer, blood pH, and — surprisingly — renal bicarbonate handling, under a `discipline='biology'` tag.
- **Bio Ch8 Immune** takes skin as a barrier (twice, using intact-vs-abraded skin as its experimental design) and antigen presentation.
- **Bio Ch9 Digestive / Ch11 Musculoskeletal / Ch4 Nervous / Ch1 Cell / Ch3 Embryo** take micturition's two-sphincter logic, bladder smooth muscle, autonomic wiring, epithelial renewal, keratin, and germ-layer origin.

What is left is genuinely rich, but it is **the tubule, not the hormone; the barrier's selectivity, not the pressure balance; the skin's water and heat physics, not its defence.**

## UNIT BREAKDOWN (57)

### Unit 1 — Renal and Urinary-Tract Architecture (4)
Cortex vs medulla and why the osmotic gradient can only exist in the medulla; the nephron segment sequence and the filtrate's itinerary; renal perfusion share and the medullary oxygen economy (the thick ascending limb's high ATP demand sitting in the least-oxygenated tissue); bladder filling as storage at low pressure (compliance), **not** sphincters and **not** smooth-muscle coupling.

### Unit 2 — Glomerular Filtration and Its Control (11)
Net filtration pressure with the capsular hydrostatic term (one computation only); ureteral obstruction → capsular pressure up → GFR down; obligatory filtration along the entire glomerular capillary with no crossover point; barrier **size** selectivity; barrier **charge** selectivity and albuminuria; the three-layer barrier and which layer's failure yields which urinary finding; filtrate-vs-plasma composition (skill 4 table); myogenic autoregulation across a mean-arterial-pressure range; macula densa NaCl sensing and tubuloglomerular feedback; granular (JG) cells as the intrarenal stretch sensor plus sympathetic input, keyed on *sensor location*; clearance as a concept (inulin clearance = GFR) and clearance above/below GFR implying net secretion/reabsorption.

### Unit 3 — Proximal Tubule and Tubular Transport (10)
Isosmotic bulk reabsorption (volume falls, osmolarity does not); filtered load = GFR × plasma concentration; transport maximum and threshold → glucosuria; osmotic diuresis from unreabsorbed solute; basolateral Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition → which segment fails and what appears in urine (keyed on nephron function, never on naming the transport class); proximal secretion of organic anions and drugs, with competition for a shared pathway; constitutive proximal water permeability contrasted against regulated collecting-duct permeability; bicarbonate reclamation as a **proximal** event probed by blocking it (keyed on segment and urine composition, never on compensating for a respiratory disturbance); urinary buffers (phosphate, ammonium) permitting acid excretion far beyond free H⁺ at pH 4.5; why urea rather than ammonia — toxicity against water cost.

### Unit 4 — Loop of Henle, the Medullary Gradient, and Urine Concentration (10)
Descending limb water-permeable/salt-impermeable → rising filtrate osmolarity; thick ascending limb salt-permeable/water-impermeable → filtrate leaves hypotonic; loop-diuretic blockade → gradient collapse → high-volume dilute urine; the multiplier itself, keyed on the *magnitude of the gradient produced*, never on "predict the consequence of counter-flowing geometry" (Ch7's whale item owns that move); vasa recta washout when medullary blood flow rises; loop length ↔ maximum achievable urine osmolarity (comparative data); urea recycling and the weakened gradient on a low-protein diet; the osmolarity profile along the nephron (skill 4 table); why a dilute urine requires an intact diluting segment; obligatory water loss — minimum urine volume for a given daily solute load (computation).

### Unit 5 — Distal Nephron, Collecting Duct, and Integrated Volume/Osmolarity Control (11)
Distal Na⁺ delivery driving K⁺ secretion, keyed on the K⁺ consequence; collecting-duct water permeability **and** an intact medullary gradient both required — final urine osmolarity with and without each; central vs nephrogenic ADH failure by site of lesion; the response to exogenous ADH after water deprivation as the discriminating data item; osmoreceptor threshold, thirst, and the direction of the renal response given measured plasma osmolarity; the osmolarity-vs-volume conflict (haemorrhage at normal osmolarity); isotonic vs hypotonic vs hypertonic fluid loss → predicted urine profile; excess free water → plasma dilution and renal handling; pressure natriuresis and the long-term volume set point (framed over days, never seconds, never the carotid sinus); aldosterone as a **stem-given** and the resulting difference between tubular-fluid and final-urine Na⁺; a diuretic site-comparison item (which segment each agent hits → predicted urine volume and composition, skill 4).

### Unit 6 — Skin: Structure and the Water Barrier (6)
Layer organisation by depth and function, keyed on the epidermis being avascular and diffusion-fed; cornification as a chemical/structural change **within** the cell (explicitly not the replace-from-below logic, which Ch9 owns); the stratum corneum and transepidermal water loss; burn injury → barrier loss → fluid and electrolyte loss and the renal response; sweat is hypotonic, so heavy sweating plus water-only replacement lowers plasma osmolarity; sweat salt loss and the kidney's compensating conservation.

### Unit 7 — Skin and Thermoregulation (5)
Subcutaneous fat as a thermal resistor (keyed on insulation as a body-composition variable, never on naming conduction/convection/radiation — Physics Ch3's deck owns the three modes); piloerection trapping still air and suppressing convection; shivering as a muscular heat effector inside a control loop (never the mitochondrial mechanism — Biochem Ch10/Ch12 own uncoupling and Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase turnover); the hypothalamic set point and negative feedback, including set-point reset; melanin's protective *placement* above the dividing basal layer.
