## Q1 — Prions and Viroids — easy (topic:easy/struct:easy) — Skill 1 — ~75s — conf 5 (g98)
**Stem:** A neurologist purifies the infectious particle from the brain tissue of a patient with Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease. Assays show the particle contains a misfolded version of a host protein but reveal no detectable DNA or RNA. Based on these findings, the infectious agent is most likely composed of:

- A. a short strand of circular RNA with no protein coat
- B. a nucleic acid genome enclosed within a protein capsid
- C. a small loop of double-stranded DNA separate from the chromosome
- D. a misfolded protein with no nucleic acid ✅

**Correct D** — openstax-microbiology.txt lines 10982-10989: "in 1982, Stanley Prusiner ... discovered that the disease was caused by proteinaceous infectious particles, or prions. Because proteins are acellular and do not contain DNA or RNA ... A prion is a misfolded rogue form of a normal protein (PrPc) found in the cell."
**Distractors:** A=adjacent_fact (Confusing a prion with a viroid (the other acellular plant agent), substituting circular RNA for protein); B=misconception (Treating the prion as if it were a tiny virus with a nucleic-acid genome in a capsid, ignoring the absence of DNA/RNA); C=adjacent_fact (Confusing the prion with a plasmid (extrachromosomal circular DNA), substituting DNA for the protein)
**Distractor citations:**
  - (Choice A) openstax-microbiology.txt lines 10925-10929: "Viroids consist only of a short strand of circular RNA capable of self-replication. ... Unlike viruses, viroids do not have a protein coat to protect their genetic information."
  - (Choice B) openstax-microbiology.txt lines 9827-9829 (Table 6.1): "DNA or RNA genome (never both)" and "Genome is surrounded by a protein capsid"; line 9958: viruses "consist of a nucleic acid (either RNA" ...
  - (Choice C) openstax-microbiology.txt line 10985: "Because proteins are acellular and do not contain DNA or RNA" — establishes the prion has no DNA, ruling out a double-stranded DNA loop (plasmid); plasmid as extrachromosomal DNA is treated in Ch.11 Microbial Genetics (horizontal gene transfer ~line 1470).

**Explanation:** This is a Biology question that falls under the content category 'Prokaryotes and Viruses (BIO)'. The answer is D because a prion is an infectious agent made entirely of a misfolded protein and contains no nucleic acid at all. The text states that prions are "proteinaceous infectious particles" and that "proteins are acellular and do not contain DNA or RNA," and defines a prion as "a misfolded rogue form of a normal protein (PrPc)" that stimulates "other endogenous normal proteins to become misfolded." Because the purified particle has a misfolded protein but no detectable DNA or RNA, it fits the definition of a prion rather than any nucleic-acid-based agent. (Choice A) A short strand of circular RNA with no protein coat describes a viroid: "Viroids consist only of a short strand of circular RNA," but the particle here contains protein and no RNA, so this is wrong. (Choice B) A nucleic acid genome enclosed within a protein capsid describes a virus, yet the assay found no DNA or RNA, so the agent cannot be a virus. (Choice C) A small loop of double-stranded DNA separate from the chromosome describes a plasmid, which is DNA, contradicting the absence of detectable nucleic acid. (Choice D) A misfolded protein with no nucleic acid matches the prion exactly, consistent with the finding of a misfolded protein and no DNA or RNA. This is a Knowledge of Scientific Concepts and Principles question because you must recall that a prion is an infectious misfolded protein that contains no nucleic acid.

## Q3 — Prokaryotic Genetics: Conjugation — easy (topic:easy/struct:easy) — Skill 1 — ~75s — conf 5 (g80)

Which donor-cell appendage is responsible for making this initial contact and drawing in the recipient?

- A. The F pilus (sex pilus), encoded by the F plasmid ✅

- D. The capsule, the gelatinous outer layer surrounding the cell

**Correct A** — openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19494-19498: "In conjugation, DNA is directly transferred from one prokaryote to another by means of a conjugation pilus, which brings the organisms into contact with one another. In E. coli, the genes encoding the ability to conjugate are located on a bacterial plasmid called the F plasmid, also known as the fertility factor, and the conjugation pilus is called the F pilus." **Distractors:** B=adjacent_fact (Naming a different filamentous surface appendage (flagellum) that serves motility rather than DNA transfer); C=adjacent_fact (Confusing the structurally similar adhesive appendage (fimbriae) with the conjugative pilus); D=misconception (Mistaking a non-appendage protective outer structure (capsule) for the transfer apparatus)
**Distractor citations:**

  - Choice C (fimbriae) - openstax-microbiology.txt lines 4884-4885: "The term fimbriae commonly refers to short bristle-like proteins projecting from the cell surface by the hundreds. Fimbriae enable a cell to attach to surfaces and to other cells." Distinct from the longer DNA-transferring pilus.

**Explanation:** This is a Biology question that falls under the content category 'Prokaryotes and Viruses (BIO)'. The answer is A because conjugation is mediated by the F pilus, the F-plasmid-encoded appendage that contacts an F− recipient, retracts to bring the two cells together, and creates the cytoplasmic bridge for DNA transfer. The text states that "In conjugation, DNA is directly transferred from one prokaryote to another by means of a conjugation pilus, which brings the organisms into contact with one another," and that the genes for conjugating are "located on a bacterial plasmid called the F plasmid... and the conjugation pilus is called the F pilus" (lines 19494-19498). It further describes that "the F pilus of an F+ cell comes into contact with an F– cell and retracts, bringing the two cell envelopes into contact" (lines 19503-19504), exactly the situation in the stem. (Choice B) The flagellum is the appendage responsible for motility, not gene transfer; the chapter on bacterial structure treats flagella as organs of "movement" alongside fimbriae and pili (line 4879), so it is an appendage but the wrong one. (Choice C) Fimbriae are structurally similar to pili but are "short bristle-like proteins" that "enable a cell to attach to surfaces and to other cells" for colonization, not the longer appendage that conducts conjugative DNA transfer (lines 4884-4885). (Choice D) The capsule is a protective gelatinous outer layer, not a filamentous appendage, and plays no role in extending toward or drawing in a recipient cell. This is a Knowledge of Scientific Concepts and Principles question because you must recall that the F pilus, encoded by the F plasmid, is the surface appendage that mediates cell-to-cell contact during bacterial conjugation.

## Q4 — Prokaryotic Genetics: Conjugation — medium (topic:medium/struct:medium) — Skill 2 — ~105s — conf 5 (g81)
**Stem:** A microbiologist mixes a pure culture of F+ Escherichia coli with a pure culture of F− E. coli that lacks the F plasmid and allows conjugation to run to completion. A student is asked to predict the donor/recipient status of a cell drawn from the original F− population after it has received DNA through the conjugation pilus. Which prediction is best supported?

- A. It remains F− because the F plasmid is retained by the original donor and only a transient cytoplasmic bridge, not a copy of the plasmid, passes to the recipient.
- B. It becomes F+ because it now carries a copy of the F plasmid and can build its own conjugation pilus, allowing it to act as a donor. ✅
- C. It becomes an Hfr cell because receiving the F plasmid causes that plasmid to integrate into the recipient's chromosome at a high frequency.
- D. It becomes F− while the original donor becomes F− as well, because the single transferred plasmid is shared so that neither cell retains a complete copy.

**Correct B** — openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19505-19508: "a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid is transferred through the cytoplasmic bridge to the F− cell, which then synthesizes the complementary strand, making it double stranded. The F− cell now becomes an F+ cell capable of making its own conjugation pilus." Also lines 19499-19500: "Cells containing the F plasmid, capable of forming an F pilus, are called F+ cells or donor cells, and those lacking an F plasmid are called F− cells or recipient cells."
**Distractors:** A=misconception (Believes only a cytoplasmic bridge (not a plasmid copy) passes, so the recipient stays F−); C=process_step_confusion (Confuses receiving a free F plasmid with the integration event that creates an Hfr cell); D=reversed_relationship (Treats transfer as splitting one plasmid between cells rather than replicative copying, so both end up F−)
**Distractor citations:**
  - Choice A (misconception): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19495-19496 establish that "DNA is directly transferred from one prokaryote to another by means of a conjugation pilus" and lines 19505-19507 confirm an actual single-stranded copy of the plasmid crosses the bridge and is made double-stranded in the recipient — refuting the idea that only a transient bridge (no plasmid copy) passes.
  - Choice C (process_step_confusion): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19520-19521: "This is because the F plasmid occasionally integrates into the bacterial chromosome through recombination between the plasmid and the chromosome, forming an Hfr cell" — Hfr status arises from integration in the donor, not from a recipient merely receiving a free F plasmid.
  - Choice D (reversed_relationship): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19505-19506: "As rolling circle replication of the F plasmid occurs in the F+ cell, a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid is transferred" — replication means the donor retains its plasmid while the recipient builds its own copy, so the plasmid is not split and the donor does not become F−.

**Explanation:** This is a Biology question that falls under the content category 'Prokaryotes and Viruses (BIO)'. The answer is B because in a standard F+ × F− cross the recipient acquires a copy of the F plasmid and is thereby converted into an F+ donor. During typical conjugation, rolling circle replication in the F+ cell sends a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid across the cytoplasmic bridge; the text states that "a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid is transferred through the cytoplasmic bridge to the F− cell, which then synthesizes the complementary strand, making it double stranded. The F− cell now becomes an F+ cell capable of making its own conjugation pilus" (Microbiology, lines 19505–19508). Because the F plasmid encodes the genes for the F pilus, a cell that gains the plasmid gains donor status (lines 19499–19500). The original donor keeps its plasmid because rolling circle replication copies the plasmid rather than handing the only copy away, so transfer is unidirectional and both cells end up F+. (Choice A) is wrong because conjugation transfers an actual copy of the F plasmid, not merely a transient bridge — the recipient does not stay F−. (Choice B) is correct: the recipient becomes F+ and can itself make a pilus and act as a donor. (Choice C) reverses the route to an Hfr cell; an Hfr strain arises when the F plasmid integrates into the chromosome of a donor before transfer, not from a recipient simply receiving a free F plasmid, and that integration is not an automatic consequence of receipt. (Choice D) describes a nonexistent "sharing" outcome; rolling circle replication means the donor retains its plasmid while the recipient builds a complete double-stranded copy, so the donor does not lose F+ status. This is a Scientific Reasoning and Problem Solving question because you must apply the unidirectional, replicative mechanism of F-plasmid transfer to predict the recipient's mating type after the cross.

## Q5 — Prokaryotic Genetics: Conjugation — medium (topic:medium/struct:medium) — Skill 2 — ~105s — conf 5 (g82)
**Stem:** A microbiologist places an F+ donor strain and an auxotrophic F− recipient strain in the two arms of a U-tube whose arms are separated by a fine filter: the filter lets the shared liquid medium pass freely between the arms but prevents any cell from crossing. No prototrophic recombinant recipients arise while the filter is in place, yet recombinants appear within hours once the filter is removed so the two populations can mingle. A student concludes that this outcome reflects a feature of conjugation that naked-DNA uptake would NOT share. Which explanation best accounts for the result?

- A. The filter degrades any free DNA released into the shared medium, so genetic material from the donor is destroyed before it can reach the recipient in either arm.
- B. The recipient is auxotrophic and therefore cannot import DNA across its envelope until it is supplied with the nutrients its donor partner secretes through the filter.
- C. Conjugation requires a pilus to draw the cells together and transfer DNA through a direct cytoplasmic bridge, which the filter blocks, whereas transformation needs only free DNA that could diffuse across the filter. ✅
- D. The shared medium dilutes the donor's F plasmid below the concentration the recipient needs to take it up, so transfer resumes only once the cells are concentrated together.

**Correct C** — openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19494-19504: "In conjugation, DNA is directly transferred from one prokaryote to another by means of a conjugation pilus, which brings the organisms into contact with one another... During typical conjugation in E. coli, the F pilus of an F+ cell comes into contact with an F– cell and retracts, bringing the two cell envelopes into contact... Then a cytoplasmic bridge forms between the two cells." Contrast with line 19372: "Transformation: naked DNA is taken up from the environment."
**Distractors:** A=misconception (Attributing DNA destruction to an inert filter, conflating the barrier with nuclease activity); B=adjacent_fact (Invoking the recipient's auxotrophy (a real, mentioned trait) as a false mechanistic requirement for DNA uptake); D=process_step_confusion (Treating conjugation as concentration-dependent uptake of free plasmid from solution, confusing it with transformation)
**Distractor citations:**
  - Choice A (misconception): openstax-microbiology.txt line 19372 defines conjugation by a pilus, not a DNA-degrading filter; line 19416 attributes DNA loss to nucleases ('environmental DNA levels are low because of the activity of nucleases'), a property of the medium/cells, not an inert size-exclusion filter.
  - Choice B (adjacent_fact): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19499-19500 state recipients are simply 'F− cells or recipient cells'; nothing ties DNA uptake to first receiving secreted nutrients, and line 19400 shows transformation uptake depends on competence, not on relieving auxotrophy.
  - Choice D (process_step_confusion): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19503-19505 describe transfer initiated by pilus contact and rolling circle replication through the bridge ('a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid is transferred through the cytoplasmic bridge'), not concentration-dependent uptake of free plasmid from the medium.

**Explanation:** This is a Biology question that falls under the content category 'Prokaryotes and Viruses (BIO)'. The answer is C because conjugation moves DNA only through a conjugation pilus that physically draws the two cells into direct contact, so a filter that blocks cell-to-cell contact while still letting the medium flow abolishes transfer—whereas transformation depends on free DNA that could diffuse across that same filter. The text states that "In conjugation, DNA is directly transferred from one prokaryote to another by means of a conjugation pilus, which brings the organisms into contact with one another" (lines 19494-19495), and that the F pilus "comes into contact with an F– cell and retracts, bringing the two cell envelopes into contact" before "a cytoplasmic bridge forms between the two cells" (lines 19503-19504). By contrast, transformation is defined as a process in which "naked DNA is taken up from the environment" (line 19372), a route that needs no cell contact and would not be stopped by a permeable filter. Because recombinants appear only when the filter is removed, the requirement being demonstrated is the direct physical contact unique to conjugation. (Choice A) The filter is described as a passive size barrier that lets the medium pass while excluding cells; nothing indicates it degrades DNA, and DNA-degrading nucleases are a property of the medium and competing cells, not of an inert filter. (Choice B) Auxotrophy means the recipient cannot synthesize a particular nutrient; it has no bearing on a cell's ability to import DNA, and conjugative transfer does not depend on the recipient first receiving secreted nutrients. (Choice C) is correct: the pilus-mediated cytoplasmic bridge demands direct contact that the filter prevents, while diffusible naked DNA would not be blocked. (Choice D) Conjugative transfer is initiated by pilus contact, not by reaching a threshold concentration of plasmid free in the medium; the F plasmid is passed cell-to-cell through the bridge, not taken up from solution. This is a Scientific Reasoning and Problem Solving question because you must apply the defining mechanism of conjugation to a novel experimental setup and predict why a physical barrier selectively abolishes one mode of gene transfer but not another.

## Q6 — Prokaryotic Genetics: Conjugation — hard (topic:hard/struct:hard) — Skill 2 — ~160s — conf 5 (g83)
**Stem:** A microbiologist mates an Hfr strain of E. coli with an F− recipient population and interrupts the mating after only a few minutes. Among the surviving recipients, donor alleles that map nearest the F-integration site are recovered at high frequency, yet nearly every recipient that acquired those alleles is still F−. Which explanation BEST accounts for both the high-frequency transfer of those chromosomal alleles and the recipients remaining F−?

- A. The Hfr donor carries its F sequences as a free cytoplasmic plasmid that is copied and transferred first, converting the recipient to F+, after which adjacent chromosomal genes follow.
- B. A bacteriophage produced by the Hfr cell packages the donor chromosomal alleles and injects them into the recipient, a viral route that moves chromosomal DNA without transferring the F plasmid.
- C. The Hfr recipient takes up the donor's chromosomal alleles as naked DNA released into the medium, a process that recombines genes efficiently but never delivers an intact F plasmid.
- D. Because F is integrated into the donor chromosome, rolling-circle transfer begins within the chromosome and moves adjacent genes across first, while the rest of the integrated F plasmid would transfer last and rarely crosses before the transient contact breaks, leaving the recipient F−. ✅

**Correct D** — openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19518-19536: "the F plasmid occasionally integrates into the bacterial chromosome through recombination between the plasmid and the chromosome, forming an Hfr cell"; "Hfr cells may also treat the bacterial chromosome like an enormous F plasmid and attempt to transfer a copy of it to a recipient F− cell. Because the bacterial chromosome is so large, transfer of the entire chromosome takes a long time ... However, contact between bacterial cells during conjugation is transient, so it is unusual for the entire chromosome to be transferred. Host chromosomal DNA near the integration site of the F plasmid, displaced by the unidirectional process of rolling circle replication, is more likely to be transferred and recombined into a recipient cell's chromosome than host genes farther away."
**Distractors:** A=reversed_relationship (Treats the Hfr donor as an F+ donor (free plasmid transferred first, recipient becomes F+) — the exact opposite of the Hfr outcome the stem describes.); B=process_step_confusion (Swaps in transduction (phage-mediated transfer of bacterial DNA) for conjugation.); C=process_step_confusion (Swaps in transformation (uptake of naked environmental DNA) for the contact-dependent conjugative cross.)
**Distractor citations:**
  - Choice A (F+ donor, reversed): openstax-microbiology.txt lines 19502-19519: "As rolling circle replication of the F plasmid occurs in the F+ cell, a single-stranded copy of the F plasmid is transferred through the cytoplasmic bridge to the F− cell ... The F− cell now becomes an F+ cell capable of making its own conjugation pilus." and line 19519: "typical conjugation in E. coli results in the transfer of the F-plasmid DNA only" — true of an F+ donor, not the Hfr donor in the stem.
  - Choice B (transduction): openstax-microbiology.txt line 19373 and 19381: "Transduction: genes are transferred between cells in a virus"; "In transduction, a bacteriophage injects DNA that is a hybrid of viral DNA and DNA from a previously infected bacterial cell" — a phage route, not the conjugative Hfr cross.
  - Choice C (transformation): openstax-microbiology.txt line 19372 and 19379-19380: "Transformation: naked DNA is taken up from the environment"; "In transformation, the cell takes up DNA directly from the environment" — no cell-to-cell contact or pilus, unlike conjugation.
**Hard-tier self-check:** Qualifies as hard on 3 of 4 gate conditions. (1) Concept-chaining (>=3 linked steps): F integrated into chromosome -> chromosome transferred by unidirectional rolling-circle starting within the integrated element -> genes adjacent to the integration site cross first/early -> the bulk of F transfers LAST -> transient mating bridge breaks before F completes -> recipient gets early chromosomal genes at high frequency yet stays F−. (2) True partial-truth trap: Choice A states real F+ biology verbatim from the text ("typical conjugation ... results in the transfer of the F-plasmid DNA only," recipient "becomes an F+ cell"), but is wrong because the stem specifies an Hfr (integrated) donor, not a free-plasmid F+ donor. (3) 510-tempting distractor: A is the canonical F+ description and snares students who don't register that Hfr means integrated F transfers last; B and C are the two other HGT mechanisms (transduction, transformation), each a plausible-sounding HGT route a strong student might rationalize. Reasoning steps: ~4-5. Wrong path each distractor punishes — A: conflates Hfr with F+, predicting recipient becomes F+ (reversed relationship); B: substitutes phage-mediated transduction for conjugation (mechanism confusion); C: substitutes naked-DNA transformation for the contact/pilus-dependent cross (mechanism confusion).

**Explanation:** This is a Biology question that falls under the content category 'Prokaryotes and Viruses (BIO)'. The answer is D because integration of the F plasmid into the donor chromosome makes the chromosome behave like one enormous F plasmid: rolling-circle transfer initiates within the integrated element and pushes the adjacent chromosomal genes across first, so genes near the integration site move at high frequency, while the bulk of the F plasmid (which would complete the recipient's conversion to F+) is positioned to transfer last and is usually lost when the fragile mating bridge breaks. The text states that the F plasmid "occasionally integrates into the bacterial chromosome ... forming an Hfr cell," that "Hfr cells may also treat the bacterial chromosome like an enormous F plasmid," and that "contact between bacterial cells during conjugation is transient, so it is unusual for the entire chromosome to be transferred." It further notes that "Host chromosomal DNA near the integration site ... displaced by the unidirectional process of rolling circle replication, is more likely to be transferred ... than host genes farther away." Because the complete F sequence transfers last, recipients usually receive only early chromosomal genes and stay F−. (Choice A) describes an F+ donor, in which "typical conjugation in E. coli results in the transfer of the F-plasmid DNA only" and the recipient becomes F+ — the opposite of the Hfr outcome and it does not explain efficient chromosomal transfer. (Choice B) describes transduction, in which "a bacteriophage injects DNA," not conjugation; no phage is involved in an Hfr mating, so this is the wrong mechanism. (Choice C) describes transformation, the uptake of "naked DNA ... from the environment," which does not require the cell-to-cell contact and pilus that define the conjugative Hfr cross. (Choice D) correctly couples the integrated location of F to both the high-frequency, position-dependent transfer of chromosomal genes and the recipients' persistence as F−. This is a Scientific Reasoning and Problem Solving question because you must integrate the integrated location of the F plasmid, the unidirectional rolling-circle order of transfer, and the transient nature of the mating bridge to predict why early chromosomal genes move efficiently yet the recipient stays F−.
