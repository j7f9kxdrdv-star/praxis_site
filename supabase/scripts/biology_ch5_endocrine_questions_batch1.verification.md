### Q6 — Hormone Classes & Chemistry (hard, Skill 2)
**Stem:** An unknown hormone is water-soluble, produces its full effect within seconds, and is completely blocked by an antagonist that cannot cross the plasma membrane; chemically, it is built by modifying a single amino acid. To which class does it most likely belong?  
**Answer:** C · **Foundation:** 3A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e, Ch 17.2: amine hormones are 'derived from the modification of amino acids' and are 'synthesized from the amino acids tryptophan or tyrosine,' including catecholamines such as epinephrine; hydrophilic/water-soluble hormones 'are unable to diffuse through the lipid bilayer' and bind 'a receptor located at the surface of the cell,' initiating a second-messenger cascade; the amino acid–derived hormone epinephrine 'has a half-life of approximately one minute' (fast action). OpenStax Biology 2e, Ch 9.1: water-soluble ligands 'cannot pass through the plasma membrane' and 'bind to the extracellular domain of cell-surface receptors,' and such ligands 'do not have to enter the cell that they affect' — so a membrane-impermeant blocker can abolish the effect only when the receptor is on the surface.  
**Distractor citation 1:** (B) OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17.2: 'Whereas the amine hormones are derived from a single amino acid, peptide and protein hormones consist of multiple amino acids that link to form an amino acid chain.' Peptide/protein hormones share the water-soluble, surface-receptor, second-messenger properties but fail the single-amino-acid clue.  
**Distractor citation 2:** (C) OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17.2: thyroid hormones are tyrosine derivatives (amino acid–based) yet are 'lipid-soluble' and bind 'intracellular' receptors 'already bound to DNA,' triggering transcription — so they are not surface-receptor-mediated, not blocked by a membrane-impermeant antagonist, and not second-scale in onset.  
**Distractor citation 3:** (D) OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17.2: steroid hormones 'are derived from the lipid cholesterol,' 'are not soluble in water (they are hydrophobic),' and 'readily diffuse through the lipid bilayer... to reach the intracellular receptor,' acting by triggering gene transcription (slow); cortisol's half-life is '60 to 90 minutes.' Steroids require a receptor but an intracellular one, contradicting the water-soluble, surface-receptor, and rapid-action clues.

### Q13 — Receptors & Signal Transduction (medium, Skill 2)

**Distractor citation 2:** (C) Incorrect: cAMP is the second messenger of water-soluble hormones (e.g., glucagon), not steroids. ### Q19 — Second Messengers & Amplification (medium, Skill 2)

### Q20 — Second Messengers & Amplification (hard, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A steroid hormone activates one intracellular receptor per hormone molecule, whereas an equal number of epinephrine molecules binding surface receptors each drive a G-protein/adenylyl-cyclase/protein-kinase pathway, yielding roughly a million-fold more phosphorylated product. Which feature of the surface pathway best accounts for this disparity?  
**Answer:** D · **Foundation:** 3A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** OpenStax Biology 2e, Ch 37 (The Endocrine System), lines 49003-49006: 'The binding of a hormone at a single receptor causes the activation of many G-proteins... Each molecule of adenylyl cyclase then triggers the formation of many molecules of cAMP. Further amplification occurs as protein kinases, once activated by cAMP, can catalyze many reactions. In this way, a small amount of hormone can trigger the formation of a large amount of cellular product.' Corroborated by OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e, Ch 17.2, lines 31580-31582: 'the phosphorylation cascade significantly increases the efficiency, speed, and specificity of the hormonal response, as thousands of signaling events can be initiated simultaneously in response to a very low concentration of hormone.' The multiple sequential catalytic (one-to-many) steps are what multiply the signal.  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B is wrong: OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17.2 (lines 31555-31562) and Biology 2e (lines 49003-49006) attribute amplification to the catalytic cascade downstream of a SINGLE bound receptor, not to the number of hormone molecules each occupying a receptor. One-hormone-to-one-receptor binding is the non-amplifying, one-to-one relationship (matching the intracellular/steroid model described in A&P 2e lines 31520-31531), so it cannot account for the disparity at equal hormone amounts.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C is wrong: OpenStax A&P 2e (lines 31555-31560) and Biology 2e (line 49006) identify cAMP as a small diffusible second messenger, and A&P 2e (line 31580) notes the cascade increases speed. However, free diffusion moves existing cAMP and increases reach/speed; it does not generate the large quantity of phosphorylated product. Amplification is the catalytic production of many product molecules, not faster diffusion of a few.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice A is wrong: OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17.2 (lines 31582-31584) and Biology 2e (lines 49007-49009) state phosphodiesterase (PDE) deactivates cAMP, ensuring the response 'ceases quickly' and 'preventing overproduction of cellular products.' PDE terminates the signal; it does not prolong cAMP or add to output, so it cannot explain the greater product yield.

### Q23 — Second Messengers & Amplification (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** A researcher applies a peptide hormone to cultured cells and then adds a drug that blocks phosphodiesterase. Compared with untreated cells, how does the drug most likely alter the duration of the hormone's intracellular effect?
