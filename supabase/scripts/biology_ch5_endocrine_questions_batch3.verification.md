### Q1 — Thyroid & Parathyroid (easy, Skill 1)
**Stem:** A gland in the front of the neck releases two iodine-containing hormones, but only after the anterior pituitary sends it a stimulating signal. Once in the blood, these hormones enter cells throughout the entire body and bind receptors on the mitochondria and in the nucleus. Which of the following whole-body parameters is established by this gland's hormones?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17: 'The thyroid hormones, T3 and T4... influence the body's basal metabolic rate, the amount of energy used by the body at rest. When T3 and T4 bind to intracellular receptors located on the mitochondria, they cause an increase in nutrient breakdown and the use of oxygen to produce ATP... an abnormally increased level of heat is released... This so-called calorigenic effect... raises body temperature.' Table 17.2 lists Thyroxine (T4)/triiodothyronine (T3) as 'Stimulate basal metabolic rate,' and Thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) from the anterior pituitary as 'Stimulates thyroid hormone release.' OpenStax Biology 2e, 37.3: 'The basal metabolic rate... is determined by two hormones produced by the thyroid gland: thyroxine... T4, and triiodothyronine... T3... T3 and T4 release from the thyroid gland is stimulated by thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), which is produced by the anterior pituitary.'  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B (blood calcium): OpenStax A&P 2e Table 17.2: Parathyroid hormone (PTH) 'Increases blood Ca2+ levels'; Calcitonin 'Reduces blood Ca2+ levels.' Blood calcium is set by PTH and calcitonin, not by the iodine-containing metabolic hormones T3/T4 described in the stem.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C (blood glucose): OpenStax A&P 2e Table 17.2: pancreatic Insulin 'Reduces blood glucose levels' and Glucagon 'Increases blood glucose levels'; Cortisol 'Increases blood glucose levels.' Thyroid hormones enhance intracellular glucose oxidation but do not set the blood glucose level.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D (blood sodium): OpenStax A&P 2e Table 17.2: adrenal cortex Aldosterone 'Increases blood Na+ levels' — an electrolyte-balance function acting on the kidney, unrelated to thyroid metabolic actions.

**Distractor citation 2:** Choice C (TRH suppressed): OpenStax A&P 2e identifies TRH as a hypothalamic hormone — 'the hypothalamus ... triggers secretion of TSH from the anterior pituitary' (line 32109) — not a pituitary hormone; and low T3/T4 relieve feedback, so TRH rises (line 32111).

### Q16 — Adrenal Glands (hard, Skill 2)

**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B — OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17: 'The most common source of Cushing's disease is a pituitary tumor that secretes cortisol or ACTH in abnormally high amounts,' the scenario of high ACTH with cortical stimulation, contrasted with the drug-driven suppression in the stem.  
**Distractor citation 2:** Choice A — OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17 negative feedback loop (Figure 17.6): elevated glucocorticoid levels cause negative signals to the pituitary and hypothalamus, lowering (not raising) ACTH.
