### Q9 — Endocrine Pancreas (medium, Skill 2)

**Answer:** C · **Foundation:** 3A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 25 (Urinary System): 'In cases of poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, glucose levels exceed the capacity of the tubular glucose symporters, resulting in glucose in the urine. OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17 (Endocrine System): 'Excessive blood glucose draws water into the urine, and as a result the person eliminates an abnormally large quantity of sweet urine. The use of body water to dilute the urine leaves the body dehydrated, and so the person is unusually and continually thirsty.'  
**Distractor citation 1:** Choice B: OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 25: 'In the case of diabetes insipidus, insufficient pituitary antidiuretic hormone (ADH) release or insufficient numbers of ADH receptors in the collecting ducts means that too few water channels are inserted into the cell membranes that line the collecting ducts... **Distractor citation 2:** Choice A: OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 17: 'Aldosterone is the major mineralocorticoid. It is important in the regulation of the concentration of sodium and...' — aldosterone governs sodium, not the glucose osmotic load in hyperglycemia.  
**Distractor citation 3:** Choice D: OpenStax A&P 2e, Ch 25: 'On the luminal (apical) surface, a Na+/glucose symport protein assists both Na+ and glucose movement into the cell' (insulin-independent secondary active transport). ### Q17 — Gonads, Pineal & Other Tissues (medium, Skill 2)
**Stem:** In a sleep study, volunteers spend the entire night in a brightly lit room rather than in darkness. Compared with a normal dark night, they report feeling alert and have difficulty falling asleep. Which change in hormone secretion best explains this outcome?  
**Answer:** A · **Foundation:** 3A · **Confidence:** 5  
**Answer citation:** OpenStax Anatomy & Physiology 2e, Ch 17.7 'The Pineal Gland': 'The secretion of melatonin varies according to the level of light received from the environment. When photons of light stimulate the retinas of the eyes, a nerve impulse is sent to a region of the hypothalamus called the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN)... From the SCN, the nerve signal is carried to the spinal cord and eventually to the pineal gland, where the production of melatonin is inhibited. As a result, blood levels of melatonin fall, promoting wakefulness. In contrast, as light levels decline—such as during the evening—melatonin production increases, boosting blood levels and causing drowsiness.' OpenStax Biology 2e, Ch 37: 'During the day photoperiod, little melatonin is produced; however, melatonin production increases during the dark photoperiod (night).'  
**Distractor citation 1:** (Choice B, reversed) OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17.7: 'as light levels decline... melatonin production increases' — the direction reverses under bright light; melatonin FALLS with light, so elevation is incorrect.  
**Distractor citation 2:** (Choice C, cortisol) OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17.6: glucocorticoids such as cortisol are secreted by the adrenal cortex under hypothalamic–pituitary (ACTH) control and 'play a role in metabolism' and stress responses; cortisol is associated with wakefulness and is not gated by retinal light through the SCN–pineal pathway that controls melatonin.  
**Distractor citation 3:** (Choice D, growth hormone) OpenStax A&P 2e Ch 17.3/Table 17.4: growth hormone (GH) is a protein hormone from the anterior pituitary that 'promotes growth of body tissues' and skeletal growth; its pulsatile release is linked to sleep, not to environmental light exposure, so it does not rise to keep a person awake.
