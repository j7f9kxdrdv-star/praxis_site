# Biology Chapter 9 — The Digestive System: Chapter Plan

## RECOMMENDED QUESTION COUNT: **75**

### How I got there

I did not start from a target and fill it. I enumerated distinct **answer-turning points** — the thing a question's key actually resolves on — that survive *both* the scope verdict and the boundary audit. Concepts that survive scope but are owned elsewhere contribute zero. Concepts that support two genuinely different keys contribute two.

That enumeration produced **80 candidate keys**. Reviewing them, **5 are marginal** — thin, low-yield, or sitting close enough to an adjacent chapter that forcing them would create exactly the collision spiral that sank Bio Ch6. Removing them gives **75**.

The five held in reserve (founder may promote any of them; each adds 1):
1. Lingual/salivary lipase (scope report itself says `commonly_tested_anyway: false`)
2. Leptin (adipose-derived — it is not a gut concept, and the gut-side framing is strained)
3. Gastrocolic reflex (near-duplicate of the enterogastric reflex item)
4. Hepatic albumin synthesis / oncotic pressure (Bio Ch7 owns Starling forces)
5. Duodenal iron/calcium absorption site (no outline home beyond generic "absorption of food molecules")

### Why not 90 or 100

This chapter is the most heavily raided in the bank. The boundary audit amputates, in whole or near-whole:
- **all** of lipid digestion chemistry, emulsification, micelle formation, bile-salt amphipathicity, enterohepatic recycling, pancreatic lipase, chylomicron assembly, lymph-vs-portal routing → Biochem Ch11 (a dedicated subtopic, 15+ items)
- **all** of the zymogen cascade, trypsin/chymotrypsin specificity, enteropeptidase, endo-vs-exopeptidase products → Biochem Ch11 + Biochem Ch2
- **all** of α/β-amylase specificity, disaccharide composition, glycosidic hydrolysis chemistry → Biochem Ch4
- **all** of autonomic control of gut motility and gastric secretion → Bio Ch4 (three items, including the exact "muscarinic block lowers motility" key)
- **all** of smooth-muscle cell biology, using the small-intestine wall as its own histology setting → Bio Ch11
- **all** of hepatic portal anatomy, lacteal routing, upstream bilirubin/heme catabolism → Bio Ch7
- **all** of insulin/glucagon, ADH, aldosterone, and the endocrine pancreas → Bio Ch5
- **all** of glycogen/gluconeogenesis and the urea cycle → Biochem Ch10/Ch12
- **all** of acid-base computation and the bicarbonate buffer system → GenChem Ch10
- gut flora as innate defence, and gastric acid as a microbial barrier → Bio Ch8

A naive digestive chapter is a 110-concept chapter. After the audit it is a 75-key chapter with a genuinely rich but *bounded* open territory: gut hormones, the enteric nervous system, motility mechanics, absorption transporters, gastric cell types, the exocrine pancreas, the downstream biliary/bilirubin story, and colonic water and flora.

**Applying the precedent explicitly:** Ch6 Respiratory was forced to 100 against ~85–90 real density and came back ~30% redundant, because each deduplication round in a saturated space spawns new collisions. Ch8 Immune was sized *below* the round number at 90 and landed clean. Ch9's density is 75–80. Sizing at 75 leaves a real 5-key margin so the verify/repair passes have somewhere to move an item rather than having to invent one. **Do not round this to 80, and under no circumstances to 90 or 100.**

## UNIT BREAKDOWN (75)

### Unit 1 — Architecture and Motility of the Alimentary Canal (12)
Intracellular (lysosomal) vs extracellular (lumenal) digestion; mechanical vs chemical digestion at organ level; organ sequence and where each nutrient class is first attacked; gut wall layer organization (mucosa / submucosa / muscularis externa / serosa — *layer contents only*); peristalsis vs segmentation (propulsion vs mixing); deglutition and oesophageal transport against gravity; lower oesophageal sphincter competence and reflux; pyloric sphincter as the gate on emptying; ileocaecal one-way flow; internal vs external anal sphincter (involuntary vs voluntary); mass movement and the defecation reflex; migrating motor complex / interdigestive housekeeping.

### Unit 2 — Neural and Endocrine Control of Digestion (15)
Myenteric vs submucosal plexus division of labour; ENS autonomy (short reflexes persisting after extrinsic denervation); enterogastric reflex; gastrin stimulus and target; gastrin negative-feedback shutoff by low pH; somatostatin as a gut D-cell brake; secretin triggered by duodenal acid → ductal bicarbonate; secretin's motility-damping second action; CCK triggered by fat and protein → gallbladder contraction; CCK → pancreatic enzyme secretion and slowed gastric emptying; GIP/incretin effect (oral vs IV glucose); ghrelin from the gastric fundus driving pre-meal hunger; cephalic phase; gastric phase; intestinal phase (predominantly inhibitory).

### Unit 3 — The Stomach: Secretory Cells and the Acid Environment (9)
Parietal-cell H⁺/K⁺ ATPase; alkaline tide; why the chief cell secretes a precursor (organ-level rationale only); pepsin's irreversible inactivation on entering the alkaline duodenum; mucous-neck cells and the mucus–bicarbonate barrier, with NSAID/ulcer as its failure mode; fundic receptive relaxation vs antral pump; rugae (distension) contrasted against villi (absorption); what the stomach does and does not absorb; intrinsic factor as the stomach's one indispensable secretion.

### Unit 4 — Exocrine Pancreas, Salivary Phase, and Enzyme Delivery (9)
Acinar cell vs duct cell division of labour; why bicarbonate must arrive before the enzymes can work (the duodenal pH window); ductal Cl⁻/HCO₃⁻ exchange and the cystic-fibrosis failure mode; acute pancreatitis as failure of the autodigestion safeguard; pancreatic-insufficiency steatorrhoea vs biliary steatorrhoea; brush-border enzymes as membrane-bound rather than luminal; salivary amylase inactivated by gastric acid and pancreatic amylase resuming in the duodenum; saliva composition, lubrication and bolus formation; exocrine vs endocrine pancreas contrast.

### Unit 5 — Small-Intestinal Absorption and Transport Machinery (11)
Multiplicative surface-area amplification (plicae circulares × villi × microvilli); loss of absorptive area (villous atrophy, resection) and which nutrient fails; SGLT1 Na⁺-coupled glucose/galactose uptake; GLUT5 fructose facilitated diffusion; GLUT2 basolateral exit; the basolateral Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase as the ultimate energy source for luminal uptake; PepT1 H⁺-coupled di/tripeptide uptake with cytosolic hydrolysis; lactase deficiency → osmotic load plus colonic fermentation; micellar ferry across the unstirred water layer; re-esterification in the smooth ER and chylomicron size exclusion at the capillary basement membrane; regional division of absorptive labour (jejunal bulk vs ileal specifics).

### Unit 6 — Liver, Bile, and the Biliary Tree (8)
Gallbladder concentration of bile by water reabsorption; CCK-driven gallbladder contraction coordinated with sphincter of Oddi relaxation; common bile duct obstruction and its consequences; hepatocyte conjugation of bilirubin (rendering it water-soluble for excretion); bacterial conversion to urobilinogen/stercobilin and stool/urine colour; obstructive vs haemolytic jaundice pattern; cholestasis → vitamin K deficiency → coagulopathy; first-pass hepatic processing and detoxification at organ level.

### Unit 7 — Vitamin Absorption, Water Balance, the Colon and Its Flora (11)
Haptocorrin/R-protein binding and pancreatic proteases freeing B12; intrinsic factor binding in the duodenum; receptor-mediated uptake at the terminal ileum; gastrectomy vs ileal resection as two routes to the same deficiency; fat-soluble vitamin absorption requiring bile — vitamin-specific consequence; the ~9 L/day fluid load and how it is divided between small intestine and colon; colonic water reclamation as solute-coupled; osmotic vs secretory diarrhoea; why oral rehydration solution needs glucose alongside sodium; short-chain fatty acid fermentation of fibre as colonocyte fuel; bacterial vitamin K and biotin synthesis with the antibiotic/bleeding vignette.

## DISAGREEMENTS AND UNCERTAINTIES — called out, not smoothed over

1. **The zymogen cascade is simultaneously the most in-scope and the most owned concept in the chapter.** The scope cartographer calls it "the single strongest anchor in this group — directly and explicitly named in the outline." The boundary auditor says AVOID IT, triple-owned across Biochem Ch11 Q65/Q66 and Biochem Ch2 batch4 Q24. **Resolution: boundary wins.** Ch9 gets exactly 2 organ-level items (why the *chief cell* ships a precursor; acute pancreatitis as failure of the safeguard). This is the highest collision risk in the build and should be verified against the Ch11 stems before drafting, not after.

2. **The two auditors directly contradict each other on chylomicrons.** The Bio Ch7 auditor says "OWN IT — frame the key on assembly/composition and size exclusion." The Biochem Ch11 auditor says "AVOID IT — assembly, apoprotein packaging and routing are the answer-turning point in four separate Ch11 items." **Resolution: 1 question maximum**, keyed narrowly on size exclusion at the fenestrated-capillary basement membrane, and it must be diffed against Ch11 Q8 and Q13 before it ships. If the diff is close, cut it and use a reserve item.

3. **The Ch11 auditor contradicts itself on micelles** — "AVOID amphipathicity and mixed-micelle formation outright" in one entry, "the mixed-micelle ferry step is only scenery in Ch11, so a question on micellar delivery across the unstirred water layer is defensible" in another. **Resolution: 1 question**, keyed on the *diffusion-barrier problem* the unstirred layer poses, never on micelle structure or amphipathicity.

4. **Peristalsis: scope says explicitly named and very commonly tested; boundary says Bio Ch4 owns the only peristalsis key in the bank.** **Resolution:** Ch9 keeps the mechanics angle only (peristalsis vs segmentation as propulsion vs mixing). The item will *read* adjacent to Ch4's; the discriminator must be a motility pattern, never a nerve or a transmitter.

5. **Gastric acid is squeezed from three directions and I am not fully confident 2 items survive.** Bio Ch8 owns it as a microbial barrier; Biochem Ch11 Q64 owns the acid-activates-pepsinogen inference; GenChem Ch10 owns the pH mathematics. The only clean lane left is the *secretion mechanism* (H⁺/K⁺ ATPase) and the *alkaline tide*. If either of those two proves to collide, this unit drops to 8.

6. **Intracellular vs extracellular digestion was never checked against Bio Ch1's lysosome coverage.** Neither auditor searched for it, and Bio Ch1 demonstrably owns intestinal-epithelium cell biology. The 1 allocated question must be diffed against the Ch1 lysosome and hydrolase items before drafting. **Uncertain — do not assume it is clear.**

7. **The gut-wall-layers question sits on Bio Ch11's staked ground.** Ch11 explicitly uses "the wall of the small intestine" as its histology setting in two items. The Ch9 item must key on *which layer contains what* (submucosal glands, plexus location, serosal continuity) and must not touch smooth-muscle cell properties in any option. Genuine collision risk; verify early.

8. **Three vitamin-K items risk colliding with each other inside Ch9** — cholestasis → coagulopathy (Unit 6), bacterial synthesis wiped out by antibiotics (Unit 7), and fat-soluble vitamin loss with impaired fat absorption (Unit 7). These are three different mechanisms and can coexist, but only if each key is mechanism-specific. Flag to the drafter: one each, no overlap, and they must not all appear in Batch 3 without a deliberate cross-check.

9. **Aldosterone's colonic Na⁺ action is technically unclaimed** but the auditor judged it too close to Bio Ch5's principal-cell item to be worth taking. I agree, and have keyed all colonic electrolyte items on transport physiology with no hormone in the answer. Noting it in case the founder wants to spend an item there.

10. **Surface-area reasoning is narrower here than it looks.** Biochem Ch11 owns "why surface area matters for a surface-acting enzyme." Ch9's two surface-area items must key on *absorptive capacity and its loss* (magnitude of amplification, consequence of atrophy or resection), never on the general rate argument.

### Bile and bile salts

- **Status:** TESTED_ELSEWHERE
