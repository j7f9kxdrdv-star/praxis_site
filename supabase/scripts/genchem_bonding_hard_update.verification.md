For each, she also notes the ideal angle predicted before lone-pair repulsion is taken into account. The measured value that is most likely to fall the farthest below its ideal VSEPR value is

- A. the axial \(\text{F}-\text{Cl}-\text{F}\) angle in \(\text{ClF}_3\), where two equatorial lone pairs bend both axial bonds away from \(180^\circ\) down to roughly \(150^\circ\).
- B. the equatorial \(\text{F}-\text{S}-\text{F}\) angle in \(\text{SF}_4\), where a single equatorial lone pair presses on two equatorial bonds whose ideal separation is already only \(120^\circ\), compressing them to roughly \(102^\circ\). ✅
- C. the \(\text{F}-\text{O}-\text{F}\) angle in \(\text{OF}_2\), where two lone pairs on oxygen compress the ideal \(109.5^\circ\) angle to roughly \(87^\circ\).
- D. the \(\text{F}-\text{N}-\text{F}\) angle in \(\text{NF}_3\), where a single lone pair on nitrogen compresses the ideal \(109.5^\circ\) angle to roughly \(88^\circ\).

## 25 — VSEPR & Molecular Geometry — Skill 4 (id 9d026d49)
**Stem:** A chemist has four neutral fluorides whose central atoms each bear at least one lone pair: \(\text{SF}_4\), \(\text{ClF}_3\), \(\text{XeF}_4\), and \(\text{BrF}_5\). Every \(\text{X}-\text{F}\) bond in all four is polar. Working out the molecular geometry of each from its electron-domain count, which of these molecules will most likely have a net dipole moment of zero?

- A. \(\text{XeF}_4\), because its six electron domains place the two lone pairs on opposite axial sites and leave four \(\text{Xe}-\text{F}\) bonds in a square planar arrangement whose dipoles cancel ✅
- B. \(\text{SF}_4\), because its five electron domains give a symmetric seesaw shape in which the four \(\text{S}-\text{F}\) bond dipoles cancel
- C. \(\text{ClF}_3\), because its five electron domains give a T-shaped arrangement in which the three \(\text{Cl}-\text{F}\) bond dipoles cancel
- D. \(\text{BrF}_5\), because its six electron domains give a square pyramidal shape in which the five \(\text{Br}-\text{F}\) bond dipoles cancel
