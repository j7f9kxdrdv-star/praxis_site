## Q1 — Reaction Types & Classification — easy — Skill 3 — 4E Stoichiometry (GC) (g63)
**Stem:** A chemistry student carries out the single-displacement reaction \(\text{Fe(s)} + \text{CuCl}_2\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{FeCl}_2\text{(aq)} + \text{Cu(s)}\) and is asked to explain why this metal-replacement reaction must also be classified as an oxidation-reduction (redox) reaction. Which statement best justifies that classification?

- A. Both metals keep the same oxidation state throughout, and it is the rearrangement of the \(\text{Cl}^-\) partners alone that defines the reaction as redox.
- B. The chloride ions are transferred intact from copper to iron, and this transfer of the \(\text{Cl}^-\) ions between the two metals is what constitutes the oxidation and reduction.
- C. The free element \(\text{Fe}\) enters with an oxidation state of 0 and leaves bonded in \(\text{FeCl}_2\) with an oxidation state of \(+2\), so its oxidation state necessarily changes and electrons are transferred. ✅
- D. The dissolved \(\text{Cu}^{2+}\) is oxidized to \(\text{Cu(s)}\) while the free \(\text{Fe}\) is reduced to \(\text{Fe}^{2+}\), and this pairing of oxidation with reduction makes it redox.

**Correct C**
**Distractors:** A=misconception; B=misconception; D=reversed_relationship

## Q2 — Reaction Types & Classification — easy — Skill 3 — 4E Stoichiometry (GC) (g64)
**Stem:** A student mixes aqueous barium chloride with aqueous sodium sulfate, and a white solid forms according to the balanced equation \(\text{BaCl}_2(aq) + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4(aq) \rightarrow \text{BaSO}_4(s) + 2\,\text{NaCl}(aq)\). Based on how the oxidation states of the elements change (or do not change) from reactants to products, how is this reaction best classified?

- A. A single-displacement redox reaction, because barium displaces sodium and is thereby reduced from \(+2\) to \(0\).
- B. An oxidation-reduction reaction, because barium is oxidized from \(+2\) to \(+6\) while sulfur is reduced from \(+6\) to \(+2\).
- C. An oxidation-reduction reaction, because chloride ions are oxidized from \(-1\) to \(0\) when they pair with sodium.
- D. A double-displacement (precipitation) reaction that is not a redox reaction, because every element keeps the same oxidation state across the arrow. ✅

**Correct D**
**Distractors:** A=process_step_confusion; B=reversed_relationship; C=misconception

- A. A sharp color change of an added indicator, because permanganate titrations require ferroin to mark the endpoint
- B. The disappearance of the deep purple color of the solution, because all of the added \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) has finally been reduced to \(\text{Mn}^{2+}\)
- C. The formation of a brown \(\text{MnO}_2\) precipitate, because \(\text{Mn}^{2+}\) is oxidized back to \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) at the endpoint
- D. The first faint purple tint that persists in the flask, because the next drop of \(\text{MnO}_4^-\) is no longer decolorized once the \(\text{Fe}^{2+}\) is consumed ✅

**Correct D**
**Distractors:** A=adjacent_fact; B=reversed_relationship; C=misconception
