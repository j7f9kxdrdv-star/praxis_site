## Notes / editorial decisions
- **Over-blanked source deck** — the four Piaget-stage cards each packed an age plus 3–4 features into one group, and the stimulant, REM, Stage-2, and mesolimbic cards did the same. These were split into single-concept cards, which is the main reason the deck grew from 52 source cards.
- **Metadata standardized** — topic `Psych/Soc` (no spaces) + underscore subtopic slug, matching Ch 3 and the majority convention.
- **Cross-deck boundaries (this chapter overlaps Ch 1 the most):** the language-AREA locations (Broca's = frontal, Wernicke's = temporal), the dominant hemisphere, the pineal/melatonin/circadian mechanism, and the EEG-as-a-tool definition are all left to Ch 1 — this deck teaches the aphasia SYNDROMES (Broca's nonfluent / Wernicke's fluent-nonsensical / conduction), the wave-per-sleep-stage detail, and the behavioral sleep cycle. Piaget, the mesolimbic reward pathway, and attention were confirmed absent from Ch 1/Ch 2 and belong here. Drug cards describe how a drug CLASS acts on a neurotransmitter, not the neurotransmitter's general function (Ch 1).
- **Matched-pair leak guarding** — dense with confusable pairs (deductive/inductive, availability/representativeness heuristic, mental set/functional fixedness, selective/divided attention, NREM/REM, dyssomnia/parasomnia, the drug classes, the language-development theories, Broca's/Wernicke's aphasia, the EEG wave-per-stage set); each contrast is co-grouped or blanked on both sides.
- **AAMC-gap additions** — availability & representativeness heuristics and overconfidence/belief-perseverance biases (beyond the summary's headline 'heuristics/biases'); the deductive-vs-inductive distinction; and the aphasia-syndrome distinctions.
